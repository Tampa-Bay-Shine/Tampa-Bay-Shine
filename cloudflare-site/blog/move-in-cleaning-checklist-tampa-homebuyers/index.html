Move-In Cleaning Checklist for Tampa Homebuyers: What to Clean Before Unpacking

Get your newly purchased Tampa-area home ready before boxes, furniture and everyday life make detailed cleaning harder.

Tampa Bay Shine field guidePractical move-in planning for Tampa, Brandon, Riverview, Wesley Chapel and nearby service areas.
Quick answer

The best time to clean a newly purchased home is after repairs and dusty work are finished, but before furniture and boxes arrive. Start with bathrooms, the kitchen, empty cabinets and drawers, closets, reachable ledges, and floors. Confirm which appliance interiors, storage interiors and detail tasks are included in your booked cleaning instead of assuming every company uses the same checklist.

When should you schedule move-in cleaning?

Schedule your cleaning after closing, once you have access and any painters, contractors or repair crews have finished. Aim to have the home empty or mostly empty. This makes cabinet interiors, closet shelves, floor edges and appliance areas easier to reach.

For a typical handoff, a practical sequence is: finish repairs; remove construction dust or debris requiring specialty cleanup; complete the move-in clean; allow surfaces to dry; then bring in furniture and unpack. If your moving truck arrives the same day, build in enough time for the booked scope and drying rather than assuming the clean can be rushed.

Room-by-room move-in cleaning checklist

Kitchen: clean before stocking food and dishes

  • Wipe accessible counters, backsplash, sink and faucet.
  • Inspect the stovetop and exterior appliance surfaces.
  • Empty cabinets and drawers before requesting interior wipe-downs.
  • Confirm whether refrigerator and oven interiors are included in your selected service.
  • Check reachable shelves, handles, switches and floor edges.
  • Let cleaned food-contact surfaces dry before putting items away.

Bathrooms: prioritize the spaces you will use first

  • Clean toilets, sinks, faucets, countertops and mirrors.
  • Address shower walls, tubs, doors and accessible fixtures according to scope.
  • Check medicine cabinets and vanity interiors if those tasks are included.
  • Look for mineral residue or soap buildup and flag heavy conditions in advance.
  • Inspect floor corners, baseboards and reachable touchpoints.

Bedrooms and closets: take advantage of empty rooms

  • Dust accessible horizontal surfaces and reachable ledges.
  • Clean empty closet shelves and built-ins if included.
  • Wipe accessible door handles and light switches.
  • Vacuum or mop the full accessible floor before beds and dressers arrive.

Living spaces, entries and floors

  • Dust accessible trim, window sills and baseboards within the selected scope.
  • Check interior glass and window tracks separately; they may require add-ons or a different service.
  • Clean floors after higher surfaces so settled dust is removed last.
  • Pay attention to entryways, stair landings and traffic routes used by movers.

What to inspect in a newly purchased home

A home may look clean during a showing but reveal dust, cabinet crumbs, pet hair or bathroom residue once it is empty. Walk through with the lights on and note any areas that need extra attention. Photograph unusual buildup before booking so the cleaning scope can be assessed accurately.

Cleaning does not repair damaged grout, permanent stains, water damage, mold or broken fixtures. Address those separately with qualified professionals. Do not assume an ordinary move-in clean includes construction-debris removal, carpet extraction or unsafe high-reach work.

Move-in cleaning or deep cleaning: which should you book?

Move-in/move-out cleaning is usually the best starting point for a vacant or mostly vacant property, because empty storage areas and exposed floors change the scope. Deep cleaning is often more appropriate for an occupied home that needs a detailed reset. If a vacant home has heavy accumulated grease, bathroom buildup or neglected conditions, ask about Deep Move-In/Out Cleaning rather than expecting a routine vacancy clean to cover it.

Compare the actual task list on the Tampa Bay Shine cleaning checklist before selecting a service. The condition of the property, not just the fact that you are moving, determines the right level.

Before your cleaning appointment

  1. Confirm access: provide the correct address, entry code, lockbox, parking and community-gate instructions.
  2. Keep utilities on: cleaners need working water, electricity and adequate lighting.
  3. Finish dusty work first: painting, repairs and contractor visits can undo a fresh clean.
  4. Empty storage: remove items from cabinets, drawers and closets you want cleaned inside.
  5. Identify priorities: explain any unusually dirty areas and confirm requested extras before the appointment.
  6. Protect the schedule: avoid overlapping movers and cleaning crews in the same rooms.

After the cleaning: a five-minute walkthrough

Before unpacking, check the kitchen, bathrooms, cabinet shelves included in the service, closets and floors. Confirm that surfaces are dry and accessible. If something within the booked scope was missed, contact the cleaning provider promptly under its service policy. Save any closing or handoff documentation separately from your cleaning receipt.

Move-in cleaning FAQ for Tampa homebuyers

Should I clean before or after moving furniture in?

Before, whenever possible. An empty home gives the cleaner better access to floors, closets and storage interiors.

Does move-in cleaning include the inside of cabinets and appliances?

It depends on the selected service and its checklist. Empty the spaces first and confirm appliance interiors and cabinet interiors when booking.

What if the seller already had the house cleaned?

Inspect the property first. A maintained, clean home may need only a targeted refresh; visible buildup or overlooked interior spaces may justify a more detailed service.

Can I book cleaning before I close?

Arrange the appointment for a time when you have authorized access and can provide entry instructions. Avoid scheduling before possession is confirmed.
